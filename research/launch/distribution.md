# C. Distribution and deploy

Area C of Michael's launch-risk brief (Nov 21, 2026): signing, install, updates, the cloud, sign-in, limits, domain and email.

- Code read: app `origin/michael/v2-integration` (d959e0e9, Nov 21), engine and cloud `origin/integration/live` (ddecb79c, Nov 20), Windows `origin/adrian/windows` (e8762604) and the local `adrian/windows-store`. Published releases were read with GET-only `gh api` on Nov 21.
- Method: 8 research agents (Apple, updates, Windows, hosting, Google OAuth, AI providers, engine cost, app failure handling) plus a spot check of the three claims that most change the plan. The agents' long notes (about 260 KB, with every source) are outside the repo in the session notes folder `area-c/01-08`; this file is the summary.
- Offline only. No lesson ran through miyagi-cloud and no paid API was called. The only live traffic was two plain `GET /healthz` calls (200 in 0.17 s, `{"ok":true,"version":"0.1.0","proxy_enabled":true}`, Nov 21 20:27 UTC) and public DNS lookups.
- Security details that could be exploited are kept out of this public file. Three items found here are one-line placeholders (C38) and go to Michael directly through area A.
- Not legal advice. Rows that depend on area B (who can sign for Apple, Microsoft and Google as minors, the AI providers' age terms) say so.

## The short version

1. **The release channel is the biggest hole.** Whoever controls the GitHub account that publishes `adrian-erlikhman/miyagi` releases controls what every install is told to download. Release immutability is off, the update card trusts any github.com URL, and the sha256 it shows comes from the same place as the file (C01).
2. **Nothing on our side stops spend.** `check_quota` always allows. The only hard brakes are provider spend limits, which may not be set (C02). The default provider tiers also cap us at about $500 (Anthropic) and $250 (Gemini) a month, which 1k users pass in about a week (C24).
3. **Group installs fail.** Ten installs per network IP per hour means the 11th Mac in a classroom, club or judging hall can't finish setup, and the app tells them to check their Wi-Fi (C05).
4. **Cloud lesson builds may not work at all.** The proxy caps `max_tokens` at 4096 while the lesson builder asks for 32000 (C03), and video watching is not on the proxy's model allowlist (C26). Both need one live build on Michael's Mac to confirm.
5. **Windows is offered on miyagi.study but can't run a lesson,** and Smart App Control blocks the unsigned installer outright. Take it off the site for launch (C06).
6. **Signing is blocked on area B** (who can enroll in Apple's program), and the first signed build will break lesson venvs unless the bundled Python gets the right entitlement (C11).
7. **Google sign-in is less blocked than the brief thought.** Testing mode doesn't stop anyone, because miyagi asks only for name, email and profile (C42). The real problems are the `wssdpsdbaqtnfhoyimit.supabase.co` name on the consent screen, school accounts blocked by default, and the 404 privacy and terms pages (C30 to C32).

## Notes and sources by topic

**Apple.** The Developer Program is $99 a year. Individuals must be of the age of majority, or a parent enrolls and shares the account. Organizations need a legal entity, a free D-U-N-S number (about 5 + 2 business days), a domain email and a website ([enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/), [D-U-N-S](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/D-U-N-S/)). Only the Account Holder can create the Developer ID certificate, and the signature shows that person's or entity's legal name. Notarization uses `notarytool` (altool stopped on 2023-11-01) and needs the hardened runtime, a secure timestamp and every Mach-O signed ([notarizing](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/notarizing-macos-software-before-distribution)). With library validation off, Gatekeeper scans every Mach-O for dangling load paths, and a notarized app was rejected over a numpy `.so` in July 2025 ([forum 794080](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/794080)). Homebrew disabled casks that fail Gatekeeper on 2026-09-01, so there is no Homebrew channel until we notarize ([Homebrew 6.0.0](https://brew.sh/2026/06/11/homebrew-6.0.0/)). The engine is charged to miyagi.app as the responsible process, which is right ([Qt blog](https://www.qt.io/blog/the-curious-case-of-the-responsible-process)). Accessibility is used only by the engine's AX reader and is optional. macOS 27 (Apple silicon only) is current. The exact electron-builder config, entitlement plists, afterPack hook and two-mode `ship-mac.sh` are drafted in `area-c/01-apple.md`, ready for whoever takes the signing lane.

**Updates.** Squirrel.Mac, electron-updater and update.electronjs.org all need a real signature, so "no auto-install" in `agents/updates.md` is the right call for an ad hoc app. Sparkle 2 can update ad hoc apps with EdDSA, but it needs native work and doesn't stop the permission resets, so skip it. Once signed, use electron-updater (blockmap deltas, `stagingPercentage`) from versioned releases or the generic provider. Don't turn on electron-updater for the unsigned Windows build: it would need `verifyUpdateCodeSignature: false`, so a compromised release would install silently. Plan: `vX.Y.Z` releases from drafts, immutable, stable asset names, a signed `latest.json` with `minimumVersion` on miyagi.study, rollback by fixing forward.

**Windows.** See C06 and C37. Long term the Store is the best channel; Store policies need a fully working app, a privacy policy URL, opt-in consent before personal data goes to AI services, an age rating, a generative-AI disclosure with a report option and a reviewer test account ([Store policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)). Free OSS signing (SignPath, Certum) needs an OSI license, which neither repo has.

**Render, Supabase, GitHub Pages.** Render Starter is `0.5c-512mb` at $7 a month (renamed 2026-08-26). Supabase Free allows 50k MAU; its built-in SMTP sends only to team members (fine while sign-in is Google only); Auth rate-limit defaults are generous for launch. GitHub Pages is fine for launch (the site is 2.2 MB of 1 GB; 100 GB a month soft bandwidth); release assets have no bandwidth limit. HTTPS is enforced (Let's Encrypt, valid to 2027-02-12).

**Google OAuth.** Supabase's Google provider asks for `email` and `profile` only. Port 47217 busy ends the flow at once (no fallback). The browser wait is 5 minutes. The minimal path today is about an hour: confirm the scopes, test with a non-listed account, fill in Branding (no logo yet), Publish app. Branded "miyagi" on the screen takes the custom domain or ID-token flow (C30), Search Console, the button fix, then Verify Branding.

**Current DNS** (public lookups, Nov 21): NS at Porkbun; A and AAAA at GitHub Pages; `www` CNAME `adrian-erlikhman.github.io`; no MX, SPF, DMARC, DKIM, CAA or DNSSEC; no wildcard; no `_github-pages-challenge` TXT.
