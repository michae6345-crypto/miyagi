# Insurance a tiny, pre-revenue AI software startup can buy to cover lawsuits (status as of 2026-11-21)

Scope: which policies would defend and settle the suits miyagi is most likely to face (privacy/breach, CIPA wiretap letters, copyright/trademark, AI output errors incl. executed shell commands, teen-safety, cofounder disputes), cost, sellers, exclusions, minor-owned LLC eligibility, homeowner's coverage, timing norms.

Citation conventions: every source was accessed 2026-11-21. "(search extract)" means the fact comes from a search-engine extract of that page and I did not open the page in full; verify before quoting. Sources without that tag were opened and read. Broker and insurer pages (Vouch, Corgi, Embroker, Coalition, Corvus, At-Bay, Hiscox) are sellers with a commercial interest; trade press is secondary. No quotes were requested and no forms were filled.

## 1. Policy types and what each covers for miyagi's risk list (including AI exclusions and AI-specific products)

### Takeaway
A combined technology E&O + cyber policy (with media liability) is the core: it is where privacy/breach, wiretap-style privacy claims, software copyright, and AI-output errors can be defended. General liability adds little for a software company because of data, IP and (since Jan 2026) optional generative-AI exclusions; D&O mostly won't cover a cofounder fight. Since 2025-2026 the market has split: some carriers add broad AI exclusions (ISO CGL forms, Berkley's "absolute" AI exclusion on E&O/D&O) while startup-focused sellers (Vouch/Corix-Hiscox, Coalition) advertise affirmative AI wording.

### Cited Findings

**Technology E&O / professional liability**
- E&O "covers lapses in professional services provided by the company"; E&O and D&O are typically claims-made; "A claims-made policy needs an act, error, or omission and a claim" (David Halbreich, Reed Smith, interviewed) — [Help Net Security, 2026-09-03](https://helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/03/david-halbreich-reed-smith-ai-insurance-coverage-gaps), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Vouch says standard E&O "does not typically cover claims arising from model outputs, hallucinations, or bias" and that it can place E&O with an AI-specific endorsement "designed to close this gap"; its AI page lists IP cover for "disputes tied to training data or generative outputs," "Regulatory defense costs as AI oversight evolves," "Data Misuse Disputes," model misuse/harmful outputs, contractual liability; carrier named is Corix, "a division of Hiscox" (page undated, © 2026) — [Vouch AI page](https://vouch.us/technology/ai), accessed 2026-11-21.
- At-Bay's tech E&O covers "trade secret misappropriation, cybersquatting, improper deep-linking," source-code license violations and software copyright infringement, contractual indemnity owed to customers, and states "No exclusions for breach of warranties, guarantees, or consequential damages"; trademark infringement is not named on the page (page © 2026) — [At-Bay Tech E&O](https://www.at-bay.com/insurance/tech-eo/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Hiscox's tech coverage summary offers $200,000 of coverage for software copyright infringement resulting from professional services (search extract; date not shown) — [Hiscox PL coverage summary, tech consultants (PDF)](https://www.hiscox.com/shared-documents/PL-coverage-summary-tech-consultants.pdf), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Coalition maps "AI product performance claims" to Technology E&O "for eligible businesses" (page © 2026) — [Coalition AI coverage](https://www.coalitioninc.com/ai-coverage), accessed 2026-11-21.

**Cyber liability (first-party breach response + third-party privacy liability)**
- Coalition maps an AI-tool prompt-injection breach to Breach Response Costs, AI-generated defamatory posts to its Media Liability insuring agreement, and "AI chatbot data sharing" to an Enhanced Privacy Endorsement that is "typically sublimited"; the page says "No AI exclusions, no surprises" (© 2026) — [Coalition AI coverage](https://www.coalitioninc.com/ai-coverage), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Coalition added a Deepfake Response Endorsement (forensics, takedown, crisis comms) to cyber policies in multiple countries in Dec 2025 (search extract) — [SiliconANGLE, 2025-12-09](https://siliconangle.com/2025/12/09/coalition-expands-cyber-insurance-cover-deepfake-driven-reputation-attacks/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Corvus: class actions tied to BIPA, VPPA and pixel claims are "typically covered by third-party insuring agreements in a cyber insurance policy," but carriers respond "primarily with exclusions" and a client "may face a wrongful collection exclusion" depending on application answers and industry (last updated 2023-05-11) — [Corvus broker blog](https://www.corvusinsurance.com/broker/cyber-coverage-explained-wrongful-collection-of-data), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Coalition: standalone cyber policies "can include coverage for many different losses that don't necessarily result from a breach of security"; "Nearly three-fourths of web privacy lawsuits cited CIPA"; 77% of wrongful-collection matters "trace back to tracking tools embedded on websites" (2025-11-05) — [Coalition blog, Daniel Woods](https://www.coalitioninc.com/blog/cyber-insurance/debunking-4-myths-around-wrongful-collection), accessed 2026-11-21.
- About 70% of wrongful-collection claims reported to Coalition since 2023 were tied to CIPA (search extract) — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/cyber/wrongful-collection-claims-escalate-as-cipa-drives-new-wave-of-cyber-lawsuits-553582.aspx), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Cyber insurers are adding exclusions targeting web-tracking litigation, per insurance attorneys (search extract) — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/insurance/web-tracking-suits-draw-pushback-from-cyber-liability-insurers), accessed 2026-11-21.

**Regulatory fines and penalties (insurability in California)**
- Cal. Ins. Code § 533.5: "No policy of insurance shall provide, or be construed to provide, any coverage or indemnity" for fines, penalties or restitution in criminal actions and in Unfair Competition Law / false-advertising actions brought by the Attorney General, a district attorney, city prosecutor or county counsel; Hinshaw says the CCPA's enforcement provision is now "agnostic concerning the issue of insurability," so CCPA civil fines turn on public policy, policy wording, and facts (intentional conduct may trigger willful-act exclusions and Ins. Code § 533) (2020-03-12) — [Hinshaw & Culbertson](https://www.hinshawlaw.com/en/insights/insights-for-insurers-alert/evaluating-the-insurability-of-ccpa-fines-assessed-by-the-california-attorney-general), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Statute text — [Cal. Ins. Code § 533.5 (public.law)](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_ins_code_section_533.5), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Commentary says several California decisions treat civil penalties as uninsurable as a matter of public policy, citing Bulluck v. Maryland Casualty (penalties are not "damages") (search extract; secondary) — [JD Supra](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/will-california-law-permit-insurance-31623), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Punitive damages are uninsurable in California: City Products Corp. v. Globe Indemnity (Cal. Ct. App. 1979) and PPG Industries v. Transamerica (Cal. 1999) — [City Products (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/88/31.html); [PPG Industries (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/20/310.html), accessed 2026-11-21.
- COPPA civil penalties: FTC maximum $53,088 per violation (adjusted 2025-01-17); FTC says 2025 levels remain unchanged during 2026 (search extract) — [Federal Register, 2025-01-17](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/17/2025-01361/adjustments-to-civil-penalty-amounts); [Federal Register public inspection 2026-18853](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-18853.pdf), accessed 2026-11-21.

**Media liability (copyright, trademark, defamation in content)**
- Media liability commonly covers defamation, invasion of privacy and copyright/trademark infringement in published material; patent infringement, breach of contract and knowing/intentional violations are common exclusions; standard CGL covers only limited IP (copyright/trademark in "advertising"), and all patent cases fall outside it (search extract; multiple secondary law-firm sources) — [JD Supra IP insurance overview](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/an-overview-of-intellectual-property-82800); [ACC](https://www.acc.com/resource-library/practical-considerations-media-liability-insurance); [NAIC social media liability paper (2021)](https://content.naic.org:443/sites/default/files/Insurance%20for%20social%20media%20liability%20-%202021.pdf), accessed 2026-11-21.

**General liability (and its data/IP/AI exclusions)**
- ISO CG 21 06 (12 23 edition) adds Coverage A and B exclusions for access to or disclosure of confidential/personal information (incl. health and biometric info); the 05 14 edition also removed coverage for loss of electronic data; effect: CGL "will not respond" to breach or biometric privacy claims (search extract; secondary forms site) — [insurancexdate CG 21 06](https://insurancexdate.com/insurance-forms/CG/CG-21-06/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- CGL "Recording and Distribution of Material or Information in Violation of Law" exclusion names TCPA, CAN-SPAM, FCRA plus a catchall for laws limiting collecting, recording, transmitting or distributing information; courts split on whether it bars BIPA; commentators say insurers may argue CIPA falls in the catchall and that deliberate collection makes a CIPA violation intentional (search extract) — [Jenner & Block / Bloomberg Law](https://jenner.com/en/news-insights/publications/beware-of-loopholes-in-privacy-insurance-policies-for-california-bloomberg-law); [Carlton Fields on 7th Cir. BIPA](https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/blogs/propertycasualtyfocus/migrated/seventh-circuit-further-clarifies-application-of-cgl-exclusions-to-bipa-claims), accessed 2026-11-21.
- ISO generative-AI CGL endorsements: CG 40 47 excludes bodily injury, property damage and personal/advertising injury "arising from generative AI"; CG 40 48 excludes personal/advertising injury only; CG 35 08 excludes BI/PD under products-completed operations (2026-08-17) — [Insurance Journal, Don Jergler](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2026/08/17/881424.htm), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Edition CG 40 47 01 26 (Jan 2026); "2,369 generative AI exclusion records already in force" across 49 states by mid-2026; W.R. Berkley, Chubb, Travelers, Berkshire Hathaway and AIG had filed to adopt ISO forms or equivalents by April; "The trigger is the connection to AI, rather than the proportion of work it touched" (2026-09-16) — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/professional-liability/isos-generative-ai-exclusion-is-already-on-thousands-of-cgl-policies-589971.aspx), accessed 2026-11-21.
- The forms are optional, so one carrier may attach an AI exclusion and another may not; brokers are told to confirm whether E&O or cyber picks up what GL drops (search extract) — [Insurance Business, AI exclusions spreading](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/professional-liability/ai-exclusions-are-spreading-through-commercial-policies-brokers-warn-clients-to-check-cover-594489.aspx), accessed 2026-11-21.

**AI exclusions on E&O / D&O**
- Berkley's absolute AI exclusion (introduced 2025) is used on D&O, E&O and fiduciary products and reaches AI use or development, AI-generated content, failure to identify third-party AI content, AI policies and procedures, and AI-related duties; a Lathrop GPM partner expects E&O and D&O to be where AI exclusions are "first applied aggressively and challenged in court," and expects "rapid adoption of some version of an AI exclusion" (2026-08-17) — [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2026/08/17/881424.htm), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Berkley form reported as PC 51380, with operative phrase "any actual or alleged use, deployment, or development of Artificial Intelligence" (search extract) — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/continued-proliferation-ai-exclusions), accessed 2026-11-21.
- "AI-specific E&O exclusions are appearing more broadly across the professional liability market"; carriers "increasingly looking for documented AI governance frameworks" (2026-09-16) — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/professional-liability/isos-generative-ai-exclusion-is-already-on-thousands-of-cgl-policies-589971.aspx), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Gallagher study cited by Insurance Journal: AI-related lawsuits rose 978% from 2021 to 2025 and 137% from 2024 to 2025; copyright was 11.2% of cases, privacy-type personal-injury claims 10.2% (2026-08-17) — [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2026/08/17/881424.htm), accessed 2026-11-21.

**AI-specific (affirmative) products**
- Vouch announced "AI Insurance" in January 2024 (search extract) — [Coverager](https://coverager.com/vouch-introduces-ai-insurance/), accessed 2026-11-21. Hiscox agreed in 2025 to acquire Corix (Vouch's underwriting arm); Vouch stays an independent broker under a distribution deal (search extract) — [Insurance Business, Vouch profile](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/companies/vouch-insurance/587347/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Armilla (Lloyd's coverholder) launched standalone affirmative AI liability in April 2025 and raised available limits to $25M in Jan 2026; Munich Re aiSure is a performance-warranty-style product; Testudo writes standalone generative-AI third-party liability for US enterprises (search extract; secondary) — [Insurance Business, AI insurance market](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/cyber/does-ai-need-its-own-insurance-market-brokers-are-already-finding-out-592662.aspx); [Munich Re insure AI](https://www.munichre.com/en/solutions/for-industry-clients/insure-ai.html); [S&P Global, 2026-02](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/2/as-insurers-retreat-from-ai-risk-one-startup-plans-to-fill-the-gap-97375264), accessed 2026-11-21.

**D&O for a tiny LLC / cofounder disputes**
- Private-company D&O policies typically contain an insured-vs-insured (IvI) exclusion that bars claims by one insured against another, including former directors/officers; carve-backs (derivative suits, whistleblower, some departed-executive claims) vary and are fact-specific; a 2025 case applied a "Dilution Claims Exception" to restore coverage in a founder dispute (search extract) — [FindLaw, IvI exclusion](https://corporate.findlaw.com/corporate-governance/the-insured-v-insured-exclusion.html); [D&O Diary guest post, 2025-07](https://www.dandodiary.com/2025/07/articles/d-o-insurance/guest-post-diluting-i-v-i/), accessed 2026-11-21.

**Teen-safety / bodily-injury style claims**
- Delaware Superior Court (decision 2026-02-27, corrected 2026-03-05) held Hartford, Chubb and other insurers had no duty to defend Meta in social-media addiction suits because the conduct alleged, even framed as negligence, describes deliberate acts rather than an "accident" (search extract) — [Insurance Journal, 2026-03-23](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2026/03/23/862428.htm); [Delaware court opinion](https://www.courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=392510), accessed 2026-11-21.

### Inferences
- For miyagi's list: breach response (screenshots containing passwords or third parties' messages) → cyber first-party; suits by users or non-user third parties over captured data → cyber third-party privacy liability, but a "wrongful collection" or statutory-violation exclusion is the biggest threat because screen capture is deliberate collection; creator copyright and "Miyagi Labs" trademark disputes → media liability / tech E&O IP grant (check trademark is named, since At-Bay's page names copyright but not trademark); model-written shell command damaging a user's machine → tech E&O product-failure grant only if no AI exclusion and the policy doesn't treat it as excluded property damage; teen-safety/mental-health → mostly uninsured (E&O/cyber typically exclude bodily injury; CGL needs an "accident" and may carry CG 40 47); cofounder dispute → largely uninsured under D&O's IvI exclusion.
- COPPA/CCPA/FTC penalties should be treated as uninsured for planning in California; defense costs for a regulatory investigation are more likely covered than the fine itself.

### Gaps
- No primary specimen text found for Berkley PC 51380, Vouch/Corix's AI endorsement, Coalition's Enhanced Privacy Endorsement, or any carrier's "wrongful collection" exclusion; wording controls.
- No source found on whether tech E&O forms treat damage to a user's files or computer from executed code as excluded "property damage," or on "betterment" exclusions in tech E&O.
- No source found on war / infrastructure exclusions in small-business cyber forms (not searched within budget).
- No California appellate decision found squarely deciding insurability of CCPA or COPPA civil penalties.
- No court decision found on a CIPA-specific cyber exclusion.

## 2. Typical premiums, limits, retentions, and price drivers for a pre-revenue software startup

### Takeaway
Seller estimates put a pre-seed/seed bundle (GL + tech E&O + cyber, $1M limits) at roughly $2,000-$4,000 per year; standalone small-tech E&O averages from marketplaces run about $780-$1,330 per year. None of these is a quote for a product that captures teens' screens, and no source quantified how screenshots, minors or AI change the price.

### Cited Findings
- Corgi (insurer/seller): pre-seed & seed bundle of GL, Tech E&O and Cyber with $1M limits "may generally cost between $2,000 and $4,000 per year" for "an eligible pre-revenue or early-revenue startup," subject to underwriting, state, limits, industry; Series A $10,000-$25,000+, with D&O alone $5,000-$10,000; controls that can lower premium: MFA, EDR, phishing training, clear customer contracts, documented security and incident-response processes; no deductibles stated (2026-03-18) — [Corgi, startup insurance cost by stage](https://www.corgi.insure/blog/startup-insurance-cost-by-stage), accessed 2026-11-21.

## 3. Who sells to tiny startups, eligibility, application questions, claims-made vs occurrence

### Takeaway
Online/startup-focused sellers include Vouch (broker, Corix/Hiscox paper), Embroker, Corgi, Founder Shield, Hiscox and The Hartford direct; Coalition, At-Bay and Corvus write through brokers. None publishes a minimum-revenue rule that excludes pre-revenue firms, and none publishes an applicant-age rule; the applicant is the entity. Tech E&O, cyber and D&O are claims-made; GL is usually occurrence.

## 4. Key exclusions and traps

### Takeaway
The traps that matter most for miyagi: wrongful/unlawful-collection and statutory-violation exclusions (CIPA, screen capture), AI exclusions, prior-knowledge exclusions (the known "Miyagi Labs" name conflict), intentional-act exclusions, uninsurable fines, bodily-injury exclusions, IvI on D&O, and misrepresentation on the application.

## 5. Does a parent's homeowner's or renter's policy cover a home-based app business?

### Takeaway
Effectively no for liability: standard homeowners policies exclude business pursuits, cap business property around $2,500, and don't cover cyber losses; endorsements raise property limits but rarely fix liability, and an LLC is a separate insured anyway.

### Cited Findings
- NAIC: business-related property losses or liability are "typically excluded from a traditional homeowners policy"; many policies cap office equipment at $2,500; options are a business endorsement, in-home business policy, or BOP (2018-01-01) — [NAIC Consumer Insight](https://content.naic.org/article/consumer-insight-working-home-your-insurance-coverage), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Maine Bureau of Insurance: homeowners policies "are not intended to cover businesses" and generally exclude liability coverage (2025-07-31 brochure) (search extract) — [Maine BOI home business brochure (PDF)](https://www.maine.gov/pfr/insurance/sites/maine.gov.pfr.insurance/files/inline-files/home_business_brochure-web%20version%2007.31.2025.pdf), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Courts often uphold the business-pursuits exclusion; part-time or hobby-like activity is not a safe harbor; the "activities usual to non-business pursuits" exception is read narrowly; incidental-business endorsements may not affect the business-liability exclusion (search extract) — [IRMI, insuring the home-based business pt. 2](https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/insuring-the-home-based-business-(part-2)); [PropertyCasualty360, business pursuits exclusion](https://www.propertycasualty360.com/fcs/2015/12/28/the-business-pursuits-exclusion/), accessed 2026-11-21.

### Inferences

## 6. Can a company owned by minors (parent as manager) buy these policies?

### Takeaway
No insurer source states an applicant-age rule; the applicant and named insured is the LLC, and the application is signed by an authorized representative. California law makes minors' contracts disaffirmable and bars minors from delegating powers, so a parent manager signing for the LLC is the standard workaround.

### Cited Findings
- Cal. Fam. Code § 6710: a minor's contract may be disaffirmed before majority or within a reasonable time after — [Justia, Fam. Code § 6710](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fam/division-11/part-3/chapter-2/section-6710/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Cal. Fam. Code § 6701: a minor cannot give a delegation of power or contract regarding real property or personal property not in the minor's possession — [Justia, Fam. Code §§ 6700-6701](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2009/fam/6700-6701.html), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Nolo: nothing prevents a minor from being an LLC member; California's LLC law is silent on organizer age; minors' contract incapacity is a practical problem for member-managed LLCs (search extract) — [Nolo](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/do-llc-members-need-18-years-old-older.html), accessed 2026-11-21. A Justia lawyer Q&A states the opposite on formation age (informal, not authority) — [Justia Answers](https://answers.justia.com/question/2024/09/11/if-i-am-a-minor-member-owner-in-a-llc-ca-1030039), accessed 2026-11-21.
- California tax agency annotation: a minor cannot be a partner because partnerships require delegation of power (seller's permit context; not an LLC holding) (search extract) — [CDTFA annotation](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol2/suta/410-0067-750.html), accessed 2026-11-21.
- Next's terms require the user to represent capacity to agree and authority to bind the business (search extract) — [Next terms](https://nextinsurance.com/terms-of-service/), accessed 2026-11-21.

### Inferences
- Structure: LLC as named insured; parent manager signs the application and binds; members and managers (the founders) are insureds by definition in most E&O/D&O forms. The founders' ages are unlikely to be an underwriting question, but the parent signer must be able to answer the security and data questions truthfully.

### Gaps
- No insurer or broker statement on applicant or owner age found. No California authority found squarely on minor LLC membership under the Corporations Code (Nolo and the Justia Q&A conflict).

## 7. Is buying insurance before launch common, and what do accelerators or investors expect?

### Takeaway
Seller guidance says you don't need a full program at day one; the hard triggers are the first customer contract (GL, tech E&O, cyber) and the first priced round (D&O). For an app shipping to minors before revenue, the claims-made structure argues for binding E&O/cyber before public launch.

### Cited Findings

## Implications for miyagi

### Takeaway
Buy one combined tech E&O + cyber policy with media liability ($1M/$1M, claims-made, earliest available retro date, no AI or wrongful-collection exclusion) before the public launch to teen users; expect about $1,500-$4,000/year if accepted. Skip D&O until outside money arrives. Treat fines, teen-harm suits and cofounder disputes as uninsured and manage them through product design and founder agreements.

### Inferences (grounded in sections 1-7)
- **Before public launch (now):** LLC applies, parent manager signs. Ask a startup broker (Vouch, Embroker, Corgi, Founder Shield, or a broker with Coalition/At-Bay access) for tech E&O + cyber + media in one form. Must-have terms: affirmative AI/"technology product" coverage for model outputs (Vouch/Corix AI endorsement or Coalition-style "no AI exclusions"); no Berkley-style absolute AI exclusion; no wrongful/unlawful-collection exclusion, or at least a defense-costs carve-back; third-party privacy liability that covers non-users (people whose messages show up in screenshots); regulatory defense costs; copyright and trademark both named; earliest retro date. Rough cost $1,500-$4,000/yr (seller estimates, not a quote).
- **GL:** cheap but marginal for a software-only company because of CG 21 06, possible CG 40 47/40 48 and Coverage B IP limits; buy only when an event venue, school, or partner contract requires a certificate.
- **D&O:** add at the first priced round ($5k-$10k at Series A per Corgi). It won't pay for a cofounder dispute because of the insured-vs-insured exclusion, so a signed operating agreement with vesting, IP assignment, and a dispute clause is the real protection.
- **At first revenue or first B2B/school contract:** raise limits to whatever the contract requires, check contractual-liability cover, consider affirmative AI cover if selling to institutions.
- **Covered if terms are right:** breach response and notification; defense and settlement of privacy suits and (maybe) CIPA letters; defense of creator copyright and "Miyagi Labs" trademark claims; claims that a model-written command or wrong grading caused financial loss.
- **Likely not covered:** COPPA/CCPA/FTC/AG fines and penalties (and anything under Ins. Code § 533.5); punitive damages; intentional-conduct findings; bodily-injury/teen mental-health claims; patent; cofounder disputes; any claim tied to facts known before binding and not disclosed (the "Miyagi Labs" conflict must be disclosed).
- **What must be true about the app to get coverage and not void it:** (1) MFA on every admin account (Render, GitHub, Google, Anthropic and Gemini consoles, domain registrar) and EDR plus disk encryption on both founders' machines, because applications ask for MFA percentage and EDR; (2) data minimization: no long-term screenshot storage, short retention, encryption in transit and at rest, and an honest count of records held, including those at Render and model vendors; (3) clear, affirmative consent screens before any screen capture, an obvious pause/off control, redaction of password fields, and a notice that third-party content may be captured, since Corvus says legal input, opt-out and informed consent are what keep a wrongful-collection exclusion off; (4) no third-party tracking pixels or session replay on the site or app, since that is the main CIPA trigger insurers cite; (5) age gating with no under-13 users (or verifiable parental consent), and a teen-appropriate privacy policy; (6) "run it for me" commands need explicit per-command user confirmation, a visible command preview, and sandboxing or allow-listing, both to cut loss frequency and to avoid an "intentional" framing; (7) truthful, complete application answers (misstatements can rescind the whole policy), with knowledge qualifiers in the application where the broker can get them; (8) report any demand letter right away, because claims-made-and-reported policies have strict notice rules.
- **Biggest uncertainty:** whether any carrier will write a pre-revenue app that captures minors' screens without a collection exclusion or a sublimit. Only a broker submission answers that, and the founders have not requested quotes yet.
