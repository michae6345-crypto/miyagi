# Platform distribution, business setup with minor founders, and export/sanctions rules for miyagi (status 2026-11-21)

Scope: what it takes for a free Electron desktop overlay (macOS first, Windows, Microsoft Store build in progress) built by two California high-school students under 18 to (1) take Google sign-in (Supabase Auth, basic scopes) out of OAuth "Testing", (2) sign and notarize on macOS and decide on Sign in with Apple, (3) ship on the Microsoft Store and sign Windows builds, (4) sign vendor contracts and form a company while under 18, (5) handle contact points, and (6) comply with export controls and sanctions. Every link below was accessed 2026-11-21. "No date shown" means the page itself showed no publication or update date. Pages marked **RECENT** changed in 2026 or shortly before.

Method note: several pages were read through a summarizing fetch tool, but every vendor age or authority clause quoted in section 4 was taken verbatim from the raw page text (downloaded and text-extracted). Apple's notarization, Sign in with Apple, privacy-manifest and ScreenCaptureKit reference pages are JavaScript-rendered and could not be read. Claims that depend on them are listed under Gaps, not Findings.

## 1. Google OAuth: limits of "Testing", moving to "In production" with only openid/email/profile, brand verification, User Data Policy, and Supabase specifics

### Takeaway
"Testing" caps the app at 100 manually listed test users. The 7-day expiry of authorizations and refresh tokens does **not** apply to apps that request only basic identity scopes (openid, email, profile). Publishing with only non-sensitive scopes needs no app verification and does not trigger the "unverified app" screen or its 100-user cap. To show the miyagi name and logo, the app needs the lighter **brand verification**: a public home page and a privacy policy on a domain the team owns and has verified in Search Console, and no Google marks. Through Supabase without a custom domain, users see `<project-ref>.supabase.co`. Because `supabase.co` is on the Public Suffix List, brand verification will most likely require a Supabase custom domain under miyagi.study, which is a paid add-on.

### Cited Findings
- **Testing user cap.** Projects in Testing are "limited to up to 100 test users listed in the OAuth consent screen", and each added test user counts against that quota. Source: [Google Cloud Help, "Manage App Audience" (no date shown; ©2026 footer)](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **7-day expiry and its exception.** Test users' authorizations expire seven days after consent, and so do refresh tokens issued for `offline` access. Apps that request only basic identity scopes (email, profile or OpenID, including the OIDC equivalents) are exempt, and those authorizations do not expire after 7 days. Apps that request any other scope do not get the exemption. Source: same page, accessed 2026-11-21.
- **In production.** A published project is "available to any user with a Google Account" once you select **Publish app**. Moving to production does not by itself require verification. Showing the app's name and logo, or requesting sensitive or restricted scopes, "may be subject to verification". The "Unverified apps" warning appears only when sensitive or restricted scopes are requested before they have been verified. Source: same page, accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Non-sensitive scopes are exempt from app verification.** "If your app utilizes only non-sensitive scopes, it is not mandatory for your app to complete the app verification process." Showing the app name and logo needs the lighter "brand verification" instead. Source: [OAuth App Verification Help Center (no date shown)](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463073), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **What triggers the unverified-app screen.** It appears when an app "uses sensitive or restricted scopes" without verification, or when the scopes requested in code differ from the consent-screen configuration. Unverified apps are limited to "100 new users in total". Development and test builds, internal apps and personal-use apps are exempt. Source: [Google Cloud Help, "Unverified apps" (no date shown)](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/7454865), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **When brand verification is required.** It is required when the app is set to user type **External**, its status is **Published**, and it wants to show a logo or display name on the consent screen. It is not required for personal use, for development, testing or staging, for service-account-only access, or for Internal apps. Source: [Google for Developers, "Submit for brand verification" (no date shown)](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/brand-verification), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Home page and privacy policy rules.**
  - The home page must be "publicly accessible, and not just accessible to your site's logged-in users". It must describe what the app does and link to the privacy policy. Google Play and Facebook pages are not acceptable as home pages.
  - The privacy policy must be on the same domain as the home page, linked from the consent screen, and must disclose how the app accesses, uses, stores or shares Google user data.
  - Source: same brand-verification page, accessed 2026-11-21. A related Google page adds that "Your homepage can not be only a login page". Source: [Google Cloud Help, OAuth app verification requirements (no date shown)](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464321), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Authorized domains.**
  - The list must include the top private domains used by the home page, privacy policy and terms of service, plus the domains of redirect URIs or JavaScript origins for "Web application" clients.
  - Ownership must be verified in Google Search Console by a Google account with owner permission for the domain that is also attached to the Cloud project.
  - Sources: [brand-verification page](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/brand-verification), accessed 2026-11-21, and the [requirements page](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464321), which says "a project owner or editor must verify each authorized domain in Google Search Console", accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Project contacts.** Contacts must be kept current: "Failure to act on timely notifications about your project could result in the loss of access to Google APIs." Source: [Google Cloud Help](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464321), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Brand verification timeline.** The automated review "typically takes a few minutes". A manual review, if needed, usually takes 2 to 3 business days. A compliant result is valid for 7 days, after which the status changes to "Need to re-verify". Source: [brand-verification page](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/brand-verification), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **App name and logo rules.**
  - No Google product names or modified Google trademarks, and no impersonating "another brand or organization, including Google".
  - The logo must not use "any Google product icons, logos, or trademarks".
  - The logo on the consent screen must match the one in the verification submission.
  - Source: [Google Cloud Help, "App Identity & Branding" (no date shown)](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13804963), accessed 2026-11-21.
  - A search summary of [Google's "Manage OAuth App Branding" page](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549049) reported a 1 MB logo limit and that changing the logo on a published External app triggers re-verification. That page was not fetched directly, so verify both points.
- **Sign-in button branding.** Use "Sign in with Google", "Sign up with Google" or "Continue with Google". Do not resize or recolor the "G" logo or use a monochrome version. Show the button at least as prominently as other third-party sign-in options. Source: [Google Identity, Sign in with Google branding guidelines (no date shown)](https://developers.google.com/identity/branding-guidelines), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Google API Services User Data Policy (last updated February 15, 2024).** These duties apply to every app, including basic scopes:
  - "All permission requests must accurately represent the identity of the application"
  - "You must publish a privacy policy that fully documents how your application interacts with user data", with its URL listed in the OAuth client configuration once the app is public
  - be honest about the purpose of each request
  - "Don't request access to information that you don't need"
  - get consent to an updated policy before any new use of the data
  - "Do not misrepresent what data is collected or what you do with Google user data"
  - keep data "secure in transit and at rest"
  - use only documented APIs

  Violations can lead to revoked API access. The detailed "Limited Use" rules sit under the extra requirements for sensitive and restricted scopes. Source: [Google API Services User Data Policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Supabase and the consent screen.** Without a custom domain, "Users will see `<project-id>.supabase.co` which does not inspire trust and can make your application more susceptible to successful phishing attempts". Supabase recommends a domain such as `auth.example.com` and says "Brand verification is not automatic and may take a few business days." Source: [Supabase docs, Login with Google (no date shown)](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/social-login/auth-google), accessed 2026-11-21. This page also contained text addressed to AI agents asking them to file a feedback report. It was ignored.
- **Supabase custom domains (RECENT, updated November 18, 2026).**
  - They are "available as a paid add-on for projects on a paid plan".
  - Supabase names the case where "the project's URL is shown on the OAuth consent screen" as a reason to use one.
  - Once a custom domain is active, "OAuth flows will advertise the custom domain as a callback URL", and the custom-domain callback must be added in the provider's console, here Google Cloud.
  - Source: [Supabase docs, Custom Domains](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/custom-domains), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Public Suffix List.** `supabase.co`, `realtime.supabase.co`, `storage.supabase.co`, `supabase.in` and `supabase.net` are in the private section of the list, submitted by Supabase Security. Source: [Public Suffix List](https://publicsuffix.org/list/public_suffix_list.dat), accessed 2026-11-21.

### Inferences
- Because miyagi requests only openid, email and profile, it can be published now. No app verification, no unverified-app screen and no 100-user cap apply once published. The 7-day expiry never applied to these scopes. Supabase also issues its own session tokens, so Google refresh tokens are not what keeps users signed in (inference from how Supabase Auth works, not from a cited page).
- Until brand verification is done, the Google screen shows the Supabase project domain instead of "miyagi".
- Under the Public Suffix List, `<ref>.supabase.co` is its own registrable domain. The team cannot verify it in Search Console, and Google says each authorized domain, including the redirect-URI domain for Web clients, must be verified. So brand verification probably requires moving the Supabase Auth callback to a custom domain such as `auth.miyagi.study`, which needs a paid Supabase plan plus the add-on. No Google or Supabase page states this outright.
- miyagi.study on GitHub Pages can meet the home-page rule: public, describes the app, links a privacy policy on the same domain. miyagi.study has to be verified in Search Console by a Google account that is an owner or editor on the Cloud project.
- The name "miyagi" contains no Google marks. The bonsai logo must stay free of any Google iconography.
- The User Data Policy duties already apply while in Testing: a privacy policy covering Google user data, minimum scopes, and no misrepresentation.

### Gaps
- Supabase paid-plan and custom-domain prices were not shown in the fetched text.
- None of the Google help pages show dates.
- Google's text does not say outright whether the redirect-URI domain must be Search Console-verified for brand verification alone, as opposed to only the home-page and privacy-policy domains. The inference above rests on "verify each authorized domain".

## 2. Apple: enrollment when under 18, Developer ID signing and notarization, Gatekeeper, Sign in with Apple, Mac App Store

### Takeaway
A minor cannot be the Apple Developer Program Account Holder. Apple requires the legal age of majority, which is 18 in California, and says a parent or guardian may enroll with their own Apple Account and share it. Organization enrollment requires a legal entity, a D-U-N-S number and a person with authority to bind it. The Developer ID certificate, which is needed for notarization, can only be created by the Account Holder. Since macOS Sequoia, users can no longer Control-click past Gatekeeper and must approve un-notarized apps in System Settings. Guideline 4.8 (Sign in with Apple or an equivalent) is an App Store review rule. It does not bind a Developer ID or notarized direct download. The Mac App Store is possible in principle but demands sandboxing, App Store-only updates, privacy labels and manifests, and explicit consent before data goes to third-party AI.

### Cited Findings
- **Individual enrollment.** You must "be the legal age* of majority in your region". The footnote reads: "If you're under the age of majority in your region, your parent/guardian can enroll with their Apple Account and share their account with you. To learn more, contact us." The Apple Account must use your legal name, two-factor authentication is required, and P.O. boxes are not accepted. Source: [Apple, "Enrollment" (no date shown)](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Organization enrollment.** Requirements, from the same page:
  - "Your organization must be a legal entity that can enter into contracts with Apple. We do not accept DBAs, fictitious business names, trade names, or branches."
  - a D-U-N-S number
  - the Account Holder "must have the legal authority to bind your organization to legal agreements" and must be "the organization's owner/founder, executive team member, senior project lead, or an employee with legal authority granted to you by a senior employee"
  - a work email on the organization's domain
  - a public, functional website on that domain

  The fee is 99 USD a year, with possible waivers for nonprofits, accredited educational institutions and government entities. Source: [same page](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Seller name and D-U-N-S.** An individual enrollee's "personal legal name will be listed as the seller". An organization's legal entity name is listed instead. D-U-N-S numbers are "free in most jurisdictions". A non-owner enrolling an organization must give a reference who can confirm their authority. Source: [Apple Developer Support, Enrollment (no date shown)](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Apple Developer Program License Agreement, §3.1 (verbatim).** "You are of the legal age of majority in the jurisdiction in which You reside (at least 18 years of age in many countries or regions) and have the right and authority to enter into this Agreement on Your own behalf, or if You are entering into this Agreement on behalf of Your company, organization, educational institution, or agency, instrumentality, or department of the federal government, that You have the right and authority to legally bind such entity or organization to the terms and obligations of this Agreement. If You are a university, Your Authorized Student Developers are of the legal age of majority in the jurisdiction in which You reside (at least 18 years of age in many countries or regions), and are currently enrolled at Your university;"
  - §1.1 has acceptance "on Your own behalf and/or on behalf of Your company ... as its authorized legal representative".
  - Source: [Apple Developer Program License Agreement, web version (no date shown; includes Declared Age Range API definitions)](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/), accessed 2026-11-21.
  - The most recent dated PDF found in search was [version 20251008](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/apple-developer-program/Apple-Developer-Program-License-Agreement-20251008-English.pdf), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Developer ID and notarization.** Gatekeeper checks for a Developer ID certificate on apps distributed outside the Mac App Store. The notary service "automatically scans your Developer ID-signed software and performs security checks", and the software is given a ticket. Only the Account Holder can create the certificate: "you must be the Account Holder of your development team in the Apple Developer Program in order to generate this certificate." Source: [Apple, Developer ID (no date shown)](https://developer.apple.com/developer-id/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Gatekeeper change in macOS Sequoia.** "In macOS Sequoia, users will no longer be able to Control-click to override Gatekeeper when opening software that isn't signed correctly or notarized. They'll need to visit System Settings > Privacy & Security to review security information for software before allowing it to run." Apple also says: "If you distribute software outside of the Mac App Store, we recommend that you submit your software to be notarized." Source: [Apple Developer News, August 6, 2024](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=saqachfa), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Guideline 4.8, Login Services.** Apps that use a third-party login such as Google Sign-In "to set up or authenticate the user's primary account with the app must also offer as an equivalent option another login service". That service must:
  - limit data collection to name and email
  - let users keep their email address private
  - not collect interactions for advertising without consent

  Exceptions include apps that use only the company's own account system, education or enterprise apps that require an existing education or enterprise account, government ID systems, and clients for a specific third-party service. Source: [App Review Guidelines (no date shown)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Scope of the guidelines.** The same page explains that notarized iOS and iPadOS apps from alternative marketplaces or websites are reviewed only against guidelines carrying the "Notarization" marker. It says nothing that applies 4.8 to Developer ID Mac software. Source: [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Guideline 2.4.5, Mac App Store.** Mac App Store apps:
  - "must be appropriately sandboxed"
  - must be packaged with Xcode technologies, "no third-party installers allowed"
  - may not auto-launch without consent
  - may not "download or install standalone apps, kexts, additional code, or resources"
  - "must use the Mac App Store to distribute updates; other update mechanisms are not allowed"

  Source: [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Screen recording (2.5.14).** "Apps must request explicit user consent and provide a clear visual and/or audible indication when recording, logging, or otherwise making a record of user activity. This includes any use of the device camera, microphone, screen recordings, or other user inputs." Source: [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Privacy (5.1.1(i) and 5.1.2(i)).** Every App Store app must link its privacy policy in App Store Connect and inside the app. The policy must name what is collected and every use, confirm that third parties give equal protection, and explain retention, deletion and how to revoke consent. Under 5.1.2(i): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." Source: [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **App privacy details ("nutrition label").** This information "is required to submit new apps and app updates to the App Store". It must cover the practices of third-party partners whose code is integrated. Source: [Apple, App privacy details (no date shown)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Privacy manifests.** From May 1, 2024, uploading a new or updated app to App Store Connect requires approved reasons for listed "required reason" APIs. Commonly used third-party SDKs must carry their own privacy manifests and signatures. Source: [Apple Developer News, February 29, 2024](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=3d8a9yyh), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Monthly screen-recording prompts (press reports, August 2024).** macOS Sequoia asks users to re-confirm screen-recording permission monthly, and apps using ScreenCaptureKit are not exempt. Sources: [MacRumors, August 15, 2024](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/08/15/macos-sequoia-screen-recording-app-permissions/); [9to5Mac, August 14, 2024](https://9to5mac.com/2024/08/14/macos-sequoia-screen-recording-prompt-monthly/), both accessed 2026-11-21.
- **ScreenCaptureKit on the App Store (informal).** An Apple Developer Forums answer says many App Store apps use ScreenCaptureKit. This is forum commentary, not an App Review ruling. Source: [Apple Developer Forums thread 728140](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/728140), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Sign in with Apple through Supabase.** The web OAuth flow needs an Apple Developer account, a Team ID, an App ID with the capability, a Services ID and a .p8 signing key. Also: "Apple requires you to generate a new secret key every 6 months" for the OAuth flow. Source: [Supabase docs, Login with Apple (no date shown)](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/social-login/auth-apple), accessed 2026-11-21.

### Inferences
- **Signing requires an adult or an entity.** Neither founder can be the Account Holder until they turn 18, so neither can create a Developer ID certificate. The only routes are a parent's individual enrollment shared with the founders, or an organization enrollment where an adult with authority to bind (in practice a parent officer or manager) is the Account Holder.
- **Whose name is shown.** With a parent's individual enrollment, the parent's legal name is the seller and almost certainly the name in the Developer ID certificate. Users would see the parent's name, not "miyagi". This follows from the seller-name rule; Apple does not say it about certificates.
- **Sign in with Apple is not required for the direct download.** Guideline 4.8 is enforced in App Store review. Notarization is an automated malware and code-signing scan. Sign in with Apple matters only if miyagi goes on the Mac App Store. The 4.8 education exception would not cover consumer Google accounts.
- **Unsigned or un-notarized builds.** On Sequoia and later, users must dig into System Settings > Privacy & Security to open the app, then also grant Screen Recording. That combination looks like a malware pattern and will cost installs and trust.
- **Mac App Store.** It is technically plausible: ScreenCaptureKit is available to App Store apps and 2.5.14 contemplates screen recording. But an Electron overlay would have to be fully sandboxed, drop its own updater, add a privacy manifest and label, and ask explicit consent before sending screen content to Anthropic or Google (5.1.2(i)). Defer it until the direct-download build is signed.

### Gaps
- Apple's "Notarizing macOS software before distribution" page could not be rendered, so the exact requirements (hardened runtime, secure timestamp, `notarytool`, stapling) are not confirmed from Apple text this session.
- No Apple source was found on further Gatekeeper changes in macOS Tahoe (26).
- No Apple statement was found on whether a minor can be added as a team member under a parent's individual account, beyond "share their account with you ... contact us".
- The Sign in with Apple reference page and the ScreenCaptureKit and App Sandbox documentation could not be rendered.

## 3. Microsoft Store and Windows code signing

### Takeaway
New Partner Center accounts are free for both Individual and Company types. Individuals verify with a government ID and selfie; companies verify with a D-U-N-S number or formation documents, plus a work email on the company domain. An Individual account cannot later be converted to a Company account. The Store developer agreement has no explicit age clause but requires "authority". The Microsoft Services Agreement behind every Microsoft account says the user is of age of majority or a parent agrees to be bound. Relevant Store policies: a privacy policy is mandatory for Win32 apps (10.5.1), opt-in consent is needed before personal information goes to third parties (10.5.2), live generative AI must be disclosed (11.16), and an IARC age rating is required (11.11). Store-signed MSIX avoids SmartScreen completely. For direct downloads, Microsoft's Artifact Signing (formerly Trusted Signing) works for organizations, and for individuals only if they are in the US or Canada and have a paid Azure subscription. EV certificates no longer bypass SmartScreen, and Smart App Control blocks unsigned files outright.

### Cited Findings
- **Partner Center accounts (page dated October 29, 2026; RECENT).** From [Microsoft Learn, "Open a developer account"](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/partner-center/open-a-developer-account), accessed 2026-11-21:
  - Two account types, Individual and Company. With the new onboarding at storedeveloper.microsoft.com there are "**no registration fees** for either account type".
  - Individual accounts suit "hobbyist, amateur, school, or personal project" work. Identity is verified "with a government-issued ID and selfie".
  - "Changing a developer account from Individual to Company is **not** supported."
  - Company accounts verify with a D-U-N-S number or official business documents, plus a work email on the organization's domain. Manual review takes 2 to 5 business days.
- **App Developer Agreement v8.11 (published March 17, 2026; effective April 17, 2026; RECENT).** Verbatim:
  - Preamble: "By agreeing to these terms, you represent and warrant to us that you have the authority to accept this Agreement, and you also agree to be bound by its terms as of the earlier of the Effective Date first written above or the date on which you first accept these terms."
  - §7: "7. WARRANTIES. You represent, warrant and undertake to Microsoft that: a. You have the power and authority to enter into this Agreement and to fully perform your obligations under this Agreement; b. If you are a business or other legal entity and not an individual, then the individual entering into this Agreement on your behalf represents that he or she has all necessary legal authority to bind you to this Agreement;"
  - A trade-compliance clause requires compliance with "the U.S. Export Administration Regulations" and "sanctions regulations administered by the U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control".
  - No age-of-majority clause was found in the extracted text.
  - Source: [Microsoft Store App Developer Agreement PDF](https://go.microsoft.com/fwlink/?linkid=528905), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Microsoft Services Agreement (published July 30, 2026; effective September 30, 2026; RECENT).** Verbatim: "By creating a Microsoft account or using the Services, you accept and agree to be bound by these Terms and represent that you have either reached the age of majority where you live or your parent or legal guardian agrees to be bound by these Terms on your behalf. If you do not know whether you have reached the age of majority where you live, or do not understand this section, please ask your parent or legal guardian for help. If you are the parent or legal guardian of a minor, you and the minor accept and agree to be bound by these Terms and are responsible for all use of the Microsoft account or Services, including purchases, whether the minor's account is now open or created later." Source: [Microsoft Services Agreement](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/servicesagreement), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Microsoft Store Policies v7.20 (page dated September 14, 2026; RECENT).** From [Microsoft Store Policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies), accessed 2026-11-21:
  - **10.5.1:** "If your product accesses, collects or transmits Personal Information, or if otherwise required by law, you must maintain a privacy policy." The URL goes in Partner Center. The policy must cover what is collected and how it is used, stored, secured and disclosed, plus user controls. "Product types that inherently have access to Personal Information must always have privacy policies. These include, but are not limited to, Desktop Bridge and Win32 products."
  - **10.5.2:** personal information may be published "to an outside service or third party ... only after obtaining opt-in consent", with an in-product way to opt out later.
  - **10.5.4:** personal information must be handled "securely, by using modern cryptography methods".
  - **10.5.8:** follow children's privacy laws.
  - Products directed at children under 13 must tell Microsoft in Partner Center (advertising section).
  - **11.11.1:** obtain an IARC age rating by accurately completing the questionnaire.
  - **11.16, Live Generative AI Content:** such products must "Disclose the use of live generative AI in the metadata", "Note the use of live generative AI in Partner Center during the submission process", and make sure the AI output complies with Store policies.
- **MSI/EXE vs MSIX (page updated August 24, 2026).** From [Microsoft Learn, MSI/EXE app package requirements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/publish-your-app/msi/app-package-requirements), accessed 2026-11-21:
  - EXE/MSI submissions need a versioned HTTPS URL to a standalone, silent-install installer. "The binary and all of its Portable Executable (PE) files must be digitally signed with a code signing certificate that chains up to a certificate issued by a Certificate Authority (CA) that is part of the Microsoft Trusted Root Program."
  - MSIX benefits include "Free Microsoft code signing and CDN hosting".
  - EXE/MSI submissions have been allowed since June 2021.
- **SmartScreen (page dated May 4, 2026).** From [Microsoft Learn, SmartScreen reputation for Windows app developers](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/smartscreen-reputation), accessed 2026-11-21:
  - Store apps "are re-signed by Microsoft and carry full reputation".
  - OV or EV-signed files still warn until reputation builds, which "can take several weeks and hundreds of clean installs".
  - "EV certificates no longer bypass SmartScreen."
  - Unsigned files show "Windows protected your PC" and need "Run anyway".
  - "Smart App Control will block execution of unsigned files unless the file has a positive reputation."
  - Artifact Signing (formerly Trusted Signing) "Starts at $9.99/month".
- **Artifact Signing eligibility (quickstart dated May 21, 2026, updated November 20, 2026; RECENT).** From [Microsoft Learn, Artifact Signing quickstart](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/artifact-signing/quickstart), accessed 2026-11-21:
  - Public Trust certificates are available to organizations in the US, Canada, EU, UK, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Switzerland, Norway and Israel. "Individual developers must be located in the United States or Canada."
  - Individual validation pulls the identity from an Azure billing account of type Individual, whose legal name and address must match a government ID.
  - Organization validation needs a primary email on a domain owned by the legal entity and a business identifier, and takes 1 to 30 business days.
- **Artifact Signing FAQ (dated November 17, 2026; RECENT).** "Artifact Signing doesn't support free, trial, or sponsored Azure subscriptions." It "doesn't issue Extended Validation (EV) certificates. There's no plan to issue EV certificates in the future." Certificates carry the validated legal name; custom CN or O values are not allowed. Source: [Artifact Signing FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/artifact-signing/faq), accessed 2026-11-21.

## 4. Minors' contracts and business formation in California and Delaware

### Takeaway
In California a minor is anyone under 18 (Fam. Code §6500). A minor may contract like an adult, but the contract can be disaffirmed (voided) before 18 or within a reasonable time after (§§6700, 6710). The exceptions are narrow: obligations made under express statutory authority (§6711), and necessaries when no parent is providing them (§6712). Minors also cannot make certain contracts at all, including any "delegation of power" (§6701). Vendor terms add their own bars:
- Gemini API: 18+, and no apps "directed towards or ... likely to be accessed by" under-18s.
- Render: no one under 16.
- GitHub: 13+.
- Apple Developer Program: age of majority.
- Microsoft account: age of majority or a parent bound.
- Anthropic Commercial Terms, Supabase and Google Cloud: no age clause, but they require authority, and Anthropic's commercial terms are "not for consumer use".

No California or Delaware statute found bars a minor from being an organizer, member, shareholder or director, because the statutes say "person" or "natural person" without an age. In practice an adult (a parent) signs and manages, and founders' equity is often held by a parent custodian under the California Uniform Transfers to Minors Act (CUTMA). The IRS says there is no age requirement for an EIN, but from December 2026 Form SS-4 will collect a parent's SSN or ITIN when a minor applies. Costs: a California LLC is $70 to file, a $20 Statement of Information, and the $800 annual tax, with no first-year exemption since 2024. A Delaware C-corp is a $109 minimum filing, plus $175 minimum franchise tax and a $50 annual report, plus California qualification if it operates there.

## 5. Required contact points: privacy contact, DMCA agent, privacy@ on a GitHub Pages domain

### Takeaway

## 6. Export controls (EAR) and sanctions (OFAC) for a free, publicly downloadable AI app

### Takeaway
A free download available to anyone without restriction is "published". Published software that is not encryption software classified 5D002 is not subject to the EAR (15 C.F.R. §§734.3(b)(3), 734.7). miyagi uses only standard TLS to support its main purpose, which is tutoring and education. Under Cryptography Note 4 that likely keeps it out of Category 5 Part 2 altogether: it would be EAR99 if it were subject to the EAR, and because it is published, it is not. Even if treated as mass-market 5D992.c, the post-March-2021 rule limits self-classification reports to mass-market *components* and certain executable software, so no BIS filing appears to be needed. OFAC sanctions apply regardless of the EAR:
- **Iran and Cuba:** general licenses cover internet communications, e-learning and user-authentication software and services, with exclusions for the government and blocked persons.
- **Crimea and the so-called DNR/LNR:** internet-communications general licenses exist but were not re-verified this session.
- **North Korea:** no authorization was found, and GitHub itself offers no service there.
- **Syria:** the comprehensive program ended July 1, 2025. The Syrian Sanctions Regulations were removed from the CFR in August 2025, the Caesar Act was repealed December 18, 2025, and the State Sponsor of Terrorism designation was rescinded August 24, 2026. The EAR still controls Syria, but EAR99 items now qualify for a license exception.

The AI providers are stricter than OFAC. Anthropic's Supported Regions Policy (RECENT, effective November 12, 2026) excludes, for example, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, China, Syria, Venezuela, Belarus and five Ukrainian regions. It covers "Use by persons while physically located in an unsupported region, including users ... of otherwise supported entities". Gemini allows use, or making an app available, only "within an available region". So miyagi's proxy should geo-block anywhere not supported by the provider it calls, and always block comprehensively sanctioned places.

### Cited Findings
- **"Published" (EAR §734.7).** Software is "published" and therefore not subject to the EAR "when it has been made available to the public without restrictions upon its further dissemination". This includes "(4) Public dissemination (i.e., unlimited distribution) in any form ..., including posting on the Internet on sites available to the public". Source: [15 C.F.R. §734.7(a), eCFR current to November 19, 2026; last amended 89 FR 34698, April 30, 2024](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/section-734.7), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Exception for 5D002 encryption software (§734.7(b)).** "Published encryption software classified under ECCN 5D002 remains subject to the EAR unless it is publicly available encryption object code software classified under ECCN 5D002 and the corresponding source code meets the criteria specified in § 742.15(b) of the EAR." Source: [same](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/section-734.7), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Not subject to the EAR (§734.3(b)(3)).** Software that is "published, as described in § 734.7" is not subject to the EAR. The accompanying note: "Publicly available encryption object code "software" classified under ECCN 5D002 is not subject to the EAR when the corresponding source code meets the criteria specified in § 742.15(b)". Source: [15 C.F.R. §734.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/section-734.3), accessed 2026-11-21. §734.3(b)(1)(ii) also notes that OFAC regulations separately "implement broad controls and embargo transactions with certain foreign countries".
- **Open-source encryption notification (§742.15(b)).** Publicly available 5D002 encryption source code is not subject to the EAR. Notification to `crypt@bis.doc.gov` and `enc@nsa.gov` is now required only for "non-standard cryptography", following the March 29, 2021 amendment (86 FR 16488). Source: [15 C.F.R. §742.15](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/section-742.15), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Mass market and self-classification (§740.17).** Note to paragraph (b): "Mass market encryption software that would be considered publicly available under § 734.3(b)(3) of the EAR, and is authorized for export under this paragraph (b), remains subject to the EAR until all applicable classification or self-classification requirements set forth in this section are fulfilled."
  - Under (b)(1), items meeting Note 3 (the mass-market note) "are classified as ECCN 5A992 or 5D992 following self-classification".
  - Under (e)(3), the self-classification report "applies to "mass market" encryption components and 'executable software' ... classified under ECCN 5A992 or 5D992 following self-classification, as well as to non-"mass market" encryption commodities and software that remain classified in ECCN 5A002, 5B002, or 5D002". 'Executable software' means software in executable form "from an existing hardware component".
  - Source: [15 C.F.R. §740.17, last amended 89 FR 28599, April 19, 2024](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/section-740.17), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **BIS mass-market page (no date shown).** Items under §740.17(b)(1) "can be self-classified with an annual self-classification report". The page does not reflect the narrower (e)(3) scope quoted above, so the two conflict. The regulation controls. Source: [BIS, Mass market](https://www.bis.gov/learn-support/encryption-controls/mass-market), accessed 2026-11-21.
- **Note 4 (BIS FAQ, via search snippet).** Items whose primary function is not information security, computing, communications, storage or networking, and whose cryptography only supports that primary function, are outside Category 5 Part 2. They take the classification they would have without encryption, for example EAR99. Source: [BIS Encryption FAQs](https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/encryption-faqs.pdf), accessed 2026-11-21. The FAQ was not fully read, and Note 4 is known to be hard to interpret. BIS's [decontrol page](https://www.bis.gov/learn-support/encryption-controls/decontrol), accessed 2026-11-21, says items not described elsewhere "can be classified as EAR99".
- **Iran general license (31 C.F.R. §560.540; last amended 89 FR 43313, May 17, 2024).** From [§560.540](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/section-560.540), accessed 2026-11-21:
  - It authorizes exporting to Iran "services incident to the exchange of communications over the internet, such as ... collaboration platforms, video conferencing, e-gaming, e-learning platforms, automated translation, web maps, and user authentication services, as well as cloud-based services in support of the foregoing".
  - It authorizes software incident to those services, "provided that such software is designated EAR99, excluded from the EAR because it is described under 15 CFR 734.3(b)(3), or classified ... under ECCN 5D992.c".
  - It does not authorize exports with knowledge they are intended for the Government of Iran or blocked persons.
- **Cuba (31 C.F.R. §515.578).** It authorizes "Services incident to the exchange of communications over the internet, such as ... e-gaming and e-learning platforms, automated translation, web maps, and user authentication services" and the cloud services that support them. ### Inferences
- **Export classification.** miyagi's primary function is screen-aware tutoring and grading. Its cryptography is standard TLS from Electron/Chromium and the operating system, used only to support that function. Note 4 likely applies, making it EAR99 if it were subject to the EAR, and a free download without restriction is "published", so it is not. Even on a mass-market 5D992.c reading, an end-item app is neither a "component" nor hardware-derived "executable software", so no self-classification report or BIS email is due. A one-page internal classification memo is enough. This is a reading of the regulation, not BIS advice.
- **OFAC still applies.** Iran's and Cuba's general licenses cover e-learning and user-authentication services and EAR99, published or 5D992.c software. Downloads and sign-ups from Iran or Cuba would likely be authorized, except for government users and blocked persons. North Korea has no equivalent authorization that was found, so block it.
- **Provider terms are the binding limit.** Anthropic's and Google's region rules are narrower than OFAC. Anthropic's covers end users "physically located" in unsupported regions, and Gemini's covers making API clients available there. The practical control is a geo-IP block at the Render proxy, and optionally at Supabase sign-up, for:
  - North Korea, Cuba, Iran, Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk always
  - any country missing from the supported list of whichever provider handles the request (for Claude, that adds Russia, China, Hong Kong, Belarus, Venezuela, Syria, Kherson, Zaporizhzhia and others)
- GitHub Pages cannot geo-block downloads, so enforcement has to sit at sign-in and the proxy. A download page notice ("not available in ...") is good practice.
- **Syria** no longer needs an OFAC block, and EAR99 software can go there under License Exception SPP. Anthropic still does not support Syria, so Claude-backed features must be blocked there. Gemini allows it.

### Gaps
- The current texts of Crimea GL 9 and Covered Regions GL 22 were not fetched. Whether OFAC has designated Kherson or Zaporizhzhia as "Covered Regions" was not confirmed.
- No North Korea internet-communications authorization was found, and absence was not exhaustively confirmed.
- The BIS Encryption FAQ text on Note 4 was read only as a snippet. The Category 5 Part 2 Note 4 text in Part 774 was not retrieved.
- The terms do not say whether IP-based geo-blocking satisfies Anthropic's "physically located" standard.
