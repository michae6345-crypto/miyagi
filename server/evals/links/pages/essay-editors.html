Why I switched editors after ten years

Priya Natarajan · June 2, 2026

For a decade I opened the same editor every morning. My fingers knew its shortcuts better than my own phone number. Last spring I moved to a different one, and people keep asking why, as if I had changed religions. The honest answer is less dramatic than they hope.

It wasn't about speed

Every comparison of editors ends up as a benchmark: how fast it opens, how many milliseconds a keystroke takes. I never noticed those numbers in my day. What I noticed was friction, the small moments when the tool made me think about the tool instead of the work. Over the years those moments added up.

Three things that changed my mind

  1. My team's projects grew, and the old editor struggled to understand them as a whole.
  2. The extensions I depended on slowly stopped being maintained, one after another.
  3. Pairing with colleagues was easier when we all saw the same screen and the same layout.

None of these is a knock against the editor I left. It was built for a different kind of work, and it is still excellent at that work. I simply do less of it now. When someone tells me they love their setup, my advice is to keep it; a familiar tool you understand beats a fashionable one you don't.

What I miss

I miss the calm. The old editor did exactly one thing at a time and never offered to help. Sometimes I still type :wq into a commit message out of habit, and smile. Tools shape how we think, and leaving one is a small goodbye to the person you were when you learned it.

Will I switch again in ten years? Probably. The point was never the editor. It was the work, and the people I do it with.
